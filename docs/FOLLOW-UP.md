## Part A — the phone call (do this FIRST, before the email)

In Nigerian agency practice a call routinely achieves in five minutes what
email does not achieve in three weeks. The email below then becomes the
*written record of a conversation that already happened*, which is far more
likely to be actioned than a cold second email.

### What to say (keep it to 30 seconds)

> Good morning. My name is Usman Ahmad. I sent an email to support@ngsa.gov.ng
> on Monday the 17th requesting access to the National Mineral Occurrence
> Database, and I wanted to confirm it was received and ask who I should be
> speaking to.
>
> We are building a Nigerian mineral intelligence platform that runs on
> government data and credits NGSA for every dataset it displays. We already
> have the cadastre and the NEITI production figures — the occurrence database
> is the piece we are missing.
>
> Could you tell me the procedure? We are happy to complete any forms or sign a
> data-use agreement, and I can come to Utako in person to collect it if that
> is easier.

### Three things to get before you hang up

1. **A name and direct number** for whoever handles data requests.
2. **The procedure** — is there a form, a written application, a fee schedule?
   (If they raise a fee, listen and note it. Do not offer to pay unprompted;
   just say you will follow whatever their process is.)
3. **Permission to visit.** An in-person collection at the GeoData Centre is
   very often the fastest route — offer it.

**Then send the email the same day**, opening with "further to our telephone
conversation this morning". That single line changes it from an unsolicited
request into a documented follow-up.

## Part B — the follow-up email

**To:** support@ngsa.gov.ng
**Cc:** info@ngsa.gov.ng
**Subject:** Follow-up: request for access to the National Mineral Occurrence Database (sent 17 August)

> Dear Sir/Madam,
>
> I wrote on Monday 17 August to request access to the National Mineral
> Occurrence Database and related geoscientific datasets, and I am following up
> briefly in case the original message did not reach the right desk.
>
> *[If you called first, replace the line above with: "Further to my telephone
> conversation with ——— this morning, I am putting our request in writing as
> discussed."]*
>
> **What we are building**
> A web-based mineral intelligence platform for the Nigerian mining sector,
> built entirely on Nigerian government data. It already carries the Mining
> Cadastre Office register of 11,706 valid mineral titles and the NEITI 2023
> audited production figures for all 36 states and the FCT. Every dataset is
> displayed with its originating agency named on screen.
>
> **What we are missing**
> The one substantial gap is mineral occurrences. Your website describes the
> National Mineral Occurrence Database as covering all 36 states and the FCT
> with commodity, location, state, status, type, reserve, grade, geology and
> mineral association. **Geographic coordinates for each occurrence are the
> critical element for us**, as the platform is map-based.
>
> Without it we are relying on sparse international sources that carry roughly
> 165 Nigerian occurrence points between them. NGSA's own database would let
> the platform show Nigeria's mineral endowment as your agency has recorded it,
> rather than as foreign datasets partially describe it.
>
> **How we would like to proceed**
> We would be grateful for guidance on your procedure and any forms we should
> complete. We are happy to sign a data-use agreement, to credit NGSA
> prominently wherever the data appears, and to share the finished platform
> with your team. If it is more convenient, I am glad to come to the GeoData
> Centre at Utako in person to collect the data.
>
> If this request should be directed to a different department or officer, I
> would be very grateful if you could point me to the right person.
>
> Thank you for your time and for the work the Agency does.
>
> Yours faithfully,
> Usman Ahmad

## Part C — if there is still nothing by Friday 28 August

Escalate gently, in this order:

1. **Visit Utako in person.** Mon–Fri 10:00–15:00. Bring a letter-headed
   printout. In-person requests are logged in a way email is not.
2. **Write to the Director General**, Prof. Olusegun O. Ige, referencing the
   two earlier attempts and their dates. Keep the same humble tone; a DG's
   office routinely redirects internally.
3. **Ask the Ministry to refer you.** The Ministry of Solid Minerals
   Development supervises NGSA; a referral from them carries weight.

## What is NOT blocked

Worth remembering so the wait does not stall the presentation:

- **Mining titles** — solved. All 11,706 polygons from the MCO GeoServer.
- **Production** — solved. NEITI 2023 audited, 36 states.
- **Boundaries, protected areas, settlements, roads, footprints** — all in.

NGSA gates **occurrences, geochemistry and airborne geophysics** only. The
platform demonstrates end-to-end today without them.
